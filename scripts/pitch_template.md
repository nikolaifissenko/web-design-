# Outreach message template

Use after a demo site is live, **and verified live on the actual
`nikolaifissenko.github.io` URL, not just the local file** (GitHub Pages
serves from `main` only; a demo sitting on an unmerged branch will 404
even though it renders fine locally, check the real URL with `curl` before
sending, every time). Keep it short: the demo does the selling.

**Persona (updated 2026-08-26, refined same day): professional, full stop,
no justification clause either.** First pass: earlier drafts opened
with "I just discovered a passion for web design, it's new to me, I hope
to make it my job someday", a practicing-beginner framing. Nikolai asked
to drop that in favor of "web designer freelance... è un lavoro che
faccio con cura, perché ci credo", better, but still explaining/
justifying the job, which reads a little like someone building a case
for themselves. **Second pass, Nikolai's actual instruction: just say
"I'm a web designer." No preamble about passion or care: a real
professional states what he does and moves straight to the point.**

**Structure (updated 2026-09-02, "Version A, light touch"):** Nikolai
shared example emails from his sister's executive-coaching business
(Jen), warm, specific, structured around a short genuine reframe rather
than a flat problem statement, and asked for the same style here. Two
things from Jen's emails don't carry over and should not be copied: her
emails use emojis, but Nikolai already asked for zero emoji in this
project's outreach ("I'm a professional"); and she states a price
outright, but the standing rule here is no price in the email itself.
What did carry over: the demo link moves up, right after the greeting
(matches Nikolai's own read that "the hook is the finished website",
most owners click before reading anyway), and the specific, verifiable
observation becomes a single reframe sentence paired with something
genuinely good about the business, not just a flat complaint. The
closing gets one warmer line expressing real interest in hearing back,
before the standard "no obligation" language. Below is the current
standard structure in both languages.

---

Buongiorno, sono Nikolai, web designer freelance qui a Roma.

Le ho preparato gratuitamente una versione nuova del sito di [Nome
Attività], con le vostre vere foto:

[demo URL]

[Una sola frase che unisce il problema concreto e verificabile del sito
attuale (non si carica da telefono, dominio scaduto, testo segnaposto,
ecc.) a qualcosa di genuinamente positivo sull'attività, così che la
frase suoni come un motivo per cui vale la pena farlo, non un giudizio.
Es. "L'ho fatto perché [problema concreto], e per un'attività con [fatto
reale, storia, artigianalità] mi sembrava un peccato non avere una
presenza online che le rendesse giustizia."]

Nessun obbligo. Se il risultato le piace, mi farebbe davvero piacere
parlarne. Resto a disposizione anche solo per un parere sincero, o mi
può chiamare direttamente.

Grazie mille per il suo tempo,
Nikolai
349 101 6416

---

## Notes

- **State the role, then move on, no beginner framing, no justification
  clause either** (see persona note above). "Sono Nikolai, web designer
  freelance qui a Roma." Full stop, straight into the demo link.
- **Lead with the live link, right after the greeting** (updated
  2026-09-02, see structure note above). Most owners will click before
  reading, don't make them scroll past a paragraph first.
- **The observation is one sentence, not a complaint.** Pair the
  concrete, verifiable problem with something genuinely good about the
  business (real history, real craft, real reputation), so the email
  reads as "this deserved better", not "your site is bad." Never
  invent the positive half, it has to be something actually true and
  checkable about that specific business, same standard as everything
  else in this repo.
- **No emoji before the link or anywhere else in the email** (updated
  2026-08-28, Nikolai's call: he's a professional, the email should read
  like one, reconfirmed 2026-09-02 when adapting Jen's warmer style,
  which does use emoji, that part didn't carry over). Just the bare URL
  on its own line.
- **No price anywhere in the email** (standing CLAUDE.md rule, also
  reconfirmed 2026-09-02: Jen's emails do state a price, that part
  didn't carry over either).
- **Always sign off with the real phone number, 349 101 6416**, not a
  placeholder, so the recipient can call instead of replying if that's
  easier for them.
- If no reply in ~4-5 days, one polite follow-up, then move on.
- **For Instagram/Facebook DM outreach specifically: attach a screenshot of
  the demo, don't rely on a bare link.** A link alone often doesn't preview
  in a DM, so the recipient sees a paste of text and no idea what it points
  to. Screenshot the live demo (Playwright against the local `index.html`
  works reliably in this environment; going straight at the live GitHub
  Pages URL can hit proxy/network issues; see any `clients/<slug>/screenshots/`
  folder for a worked example) and say explicitly in the message *"nell'immagine
  ho fatto una demo di come potrebbe venire il sito"* before the link. The
  image is the pitch, the link is just for them to click through afterward.
