- **Notes:**
  - **Everything on this demo is real, zero stock.** Genuinely lucky
    case: a Blogspot blog nobody would call a "real site" turned out to
    hold real photos, a real color palette (in its raw CSS), and enough
    text to confirm history, all uploaded by the shop itself.
  - **Story, extraordinary and real**: founded 1910 by Augusto Bordi in
    the Esquilino, now at the fourth generation with Claudio Bordi and
    his wife Francesca, an art historian. Recognized "Bottega Storica di
    Eccellenza" by the Comune di Roma. During the Nazi-Fascist
    occupation, great-grandfather Augusto hid fifteen people (antifascists
    and Jews) in the cellar; all escaped detection. The shop still sells
    a natural furniture wax made from the great-grandfather's own
    handwritten recipe. Source: Il Cielo sopra Esquilino.
  - **Logo:** a real mark exists, photographed on the shop's own
    storefront sign (see `images/hero.jpg`, a real press photo from Il
    Cielo sopra Esquilino): a circular painter's-palette icon with
    colored paint dabs, above "COLORI" and "ARTICOLI PER BELLE ARTI"
    lettering. Too small and shot at an angle in the source photo for a
    clean crop into the 40x40 circular slot. Per `branding-extraction.md`,
    redesigned a clean SVG in the same spirit (a palette with colored
    dabs), colored with real values pulled from the shop's own Blogspot
    CSS (`#634320` dark brown, `#ba742a` ochre), not invented.
  - **Colors:** `#ba742a` (warm ochre) and `#634320` (dark brown), both
    pulled directly from the real `color:` declarations in the shop's
    own Blogspot theme CSS, not guessed.
  - **Photos, all real, none stock:**
    - `hero.jpg`, the shop's real storefront on Via dello Statuto, with
      the real palette-icon signage visible above "COLORI" (press photo,
      Il Cielo sopra Esquilino, cropped to remove unrelated ad posters
      at the bottom of the frame).
    - `about.jpg`, a real interior photo: a wooden paint box, open,
      showing oil-paint tubes, with "Per tutti i tuoi sogni & emozioni"
      carved into the lid, a shop employee visible in the background
      (photo from the shop's own Blogspot blog).
    - `gallery-1.jpg`, the shop's own real museum-style display vitrine,
      full of antique paint tins, bottles, and tools (same source).
    - `gallery-2.jpg`, a customer's Monet-style water-lily painting
      photographed inside the shop, filename "ninfee claudio.jpg" (same
      source), a nice detail showing real customer/course work, not
      staged.
